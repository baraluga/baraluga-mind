Yes, they're set in the DAG's code. They're constants at the top of [freshness.py](smp-india/dags/src/scripts/darwin_publish/freshness.py):

```python
MINUTE_MAX_AGE = pd.Timedelta(minutes=20)       # 1-min series
QUARTER_HOUR_MAX_AGE = pd.Timedelta(minutes=45) # 15-min series
PENDING_MAX_AGE_SECONDS = 15 * 60               # oldest batch waiting in the spool
```

**What each one decides:** "how long can this go without new data before we call it broken?" Each run of the DAG (every 15 minutes) checks:
- **20 min:** has each 1-minute series (active power per site) received a point in the last 20 minutes? Normally a point lands every few minutes, because the publisher runs every 5. Twenty minutes allows for a couple of slow publisher runs before alerting.
- **45 min:** has each 15-minute series (wind, irradiance, plus 15-minute power) received a point in the last 45 minutes? A 15-minute value only exists once its window closes and the publisher picks it up, so its normal age is already 15 to 30 minutes.
- **15 min:** has any batch been waiting in `spool/pending` for over 15 minutes? That means the collector is producing data but the publisher isn't sending it to TSDB.

**Why ops need to agree:** it's a trade-off between alert speed and false alarms:
- **Lower thresholds** alert faster, but normal hiccups (a slow publisher run, TSDB lagging briefly) start paging people.
- **Higher thresholds** mean fewer false alarms, but slower alerts. At today's values, a real failure can take up to about 35 minutes to alert (the 20-minute threshold plus up to 15 minutes until the next run).

I picked these from how the pipeline is built, not from real data, since Darwin isn't publishing yet. So "agree with ops" really means: is about 35 minutes acceptable to them, or do they need faster? If they want different numbers, it's a one-line change plus a deploy.

Yes, it can help. The **DAC connectivity** workflow ([dac-connectivity.yml](.github/workflows/dac-connectivity.yml)) is read-only and deploys nothing. For DEV, run it with:

- `environment=dev`
- `probe_atlas_hosting=true`: this is the one that matters here
- `probe_atlas_auth=true`: needed so the probe can assume the service role and read ECS/ALB state

What it will show (from [scripts/atlas_probe.py](scripts/atlas_probe.py)):

1. **Health URL**: the workflow requests `https://dashboards-dev.gems.myengie.com/dashboard-atlas/health` without following redirects. If it gets a 302 back, that confirms what Francois sees.
2. **ECS service**: desired vs running task count. If running is 0 or lower than desired, the container is crashing or was stopped.
3. **ALB routing**: whether a listener rule still forwards `/dashboard-atlas` to our target group, and the health state of each target. Being sent to the dashboards homepage usually means one of two things. Either the path rule is gone and the ALB's default action catches the request, or there are no healthy targets.

How to read the result:
- **Rule missing**: DAC changed the shared ALB config. Take that to the DAC team.
- **Rule present, targets unhealthy or 0 running**: the problem is on our side (container or health check). Check the ECS task logs, or redeploy.
- **Everything green but health still redirects**: probably the Okta/auth layer. Also a DAC question.

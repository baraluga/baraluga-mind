Your intuition about operating capacity being the physical ceiling is basically right. The misunderstanding is treating available capacity as an absolute flow level. OCCTO defines it as additional transferable headroom relative to the signed planned flow:

`Available capacity = Operating capacity − Margin − Planned flow`

Because planned flow is netted across directions, it can be negative. Subtracting a negative number makes available capacity greater than operating capacity. This is explicitly OCCTO’s formula and is calculated separately by direction. [OCCTO Business Rules, §133 / Table 10-2](https://www.occto.or.jp/assets/occto/article/gyoumukitei2604.pdf)

A concrete live-source example from the screenshot period:

| OCCTO value | Hokkaido → Tohoku, 2026-02-01 03:00 |
|---|---:|
| Operating capacity | 900.0 MW |
| Margin | 110.0 MW |
| Planned flow | −409.7 MW |
| Available capacity | 1,199.7 MW |

The arithmetic is exact:

`900 − 110 − (−409.7) = 1,199.7 MW`

That does not mean the interconnector may physically carry 1,199.7 MW forward. If the full available capacity were booked:

`existing planned flow −409.7 + new forward transfer 1,199.7 = 790 MW`

Then adding the reserved margin gives the 900 MW operating limit.

OCCTO also explicitly says forward and reverse utilization plans are netted into one planned-flow value, and confirms that Hokkaido → Tohoku is the positive direction while the reverse direction is negative. ### The main thing you might be missing: policy differences

A completely generic “compare value, then write” implementation is insufficient.


